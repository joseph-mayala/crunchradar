The problem, in our students' words

Nobody sees the whole cohort's week.

Each lecturer sets deadlines for one module. We asked 21 students in our cohort what that adds up to.

18of 21

had three or more deadlines or tests in their busiest week

Seven had four or more, and three had five or more.

20of 21

handed in work they knew was not their best because of clashes

Seven said this happens often.

16of 21

found out late about something that was not shown on Moodle

Ten said it has happened more than once.

17of 21

had an assignment due before feedback on the last one

Four said this happens often.

20of 21

said a tool like CrunchRadar would be useful

Eleven said very useful. One said not useful.

14of 21

would read a Monday email with their week ahead

Seven more said maybe. None said no.

Source [6]: CrunchRadar student survey, 21 responses, 12 to 13 November 2026, shared in course group chats. A convenience sample, not a random one. The two usefulness questions describe CrunchRadar first, so they show interest, not proof of use. Data and method: docs/survey in the repository.
CrunchRadar03
Research

The limit comes from a rule, not a guess.

How we set the weekly ceiling
21 credits x 40 h (MQA: 1 credit = 40 hours) [1]840 h
Spread over 14 weeks60 h
Minus classes (our assumption)- 20 h
Minus revision and reading (our assumption)- 12 h
Assignment work per week28 h
So the result does not hang on one number

Crunch weeks on the sample cohort at other limits. At 28 h there are 3.

Existing Moodle tools such as Workload Radar [2] and Brickfield's Assessment Bundle [3] show deadlines across modules but count items, not hours, and propose nothing. Universities such as Hertfordshire ask staff to limit assessment bunching by hand [4]. CrunchRadar adds the hours, the proposal and the approval.

Sources [1] to [4] are listed in full on the last slide. The two deductions (classes, revision) are our assumptions and are editable in the app.
CrunchRadar04
The solution

One loop, every Monday. A person decides at step three.

1

Detect

Reads the calendar feed and the briefs, and adds up the hours per week.

→
2

Propose

Suggests a move the rules allow: never earlier, never a test, never a festive week.

→
3

Approve

A named person decides through a single-use link that expires in 72 hours.

→
4

Notify

The lecturer hears only after approval. A follow-up checks the calendar and reminds twice.

Runs on a schedule: Monday 08:00 and 08:30 Malaysia time, with catch-up if the machine was off. The festive-week rule follows practice at universities such as Monash Malaysia, which keeps timed assessments off weekends and public holidays [5].
CrunchRadar05
The agent

The model can suggest. It cannot decide.

One real Monday run, step by step
get_overviewRead 14 weeks of hours. 3 are over the 28 h limit.
foundA group demo in Week 12, inside a brief. Not on the calendar.
get_optionsAsked for the moves the rules allow. It cannot propose any other.
request_approvalSWAT Architecture report, Week 11 to Week 13. Sent to the programme leader.
finishNothing has changed yet. A person decides.

Guardrails are code

Rules, not instructions to the model. A move that breaks one is never offered, and the model's text is stripped of links before it reaches an email.

Never fails silently

If Gemini is busy or rate limited, a rules planner runs the same steps and the run says so with a RULES badge.

Tool names are real: weekly_summary, list_allowed_moves, request_approval, check_draft_assignment are also served over MCP.

CrunchRadar07
How it is built

Built to plug into the tools a university already uses.

Connectors
Today (stand-in)Becomes
Calendar feed file  Google Calendar
Briefs folder  Google Drive
Email outbox / SMTP  Microsoft Outlook
Agent loop on the Gemini API  Gemini Enterprise

 MCP tools and OpenAPI

The rules are served as four tools. An agent built in Gemini Enterprise can call them. None can change a deadline.

125 automated tests

68 backend and 57 frontend. A scheduler with catch-up, single-use links, and a follow-up check are all tested.
