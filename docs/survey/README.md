# Student survey: "Deadlines and workload: 1-minute survey"

**21 responses, collected 12 to 13 November 2026**, from students in our cohort, through course group chats. It is a convenience sample, not a random one, so we report counts out of 21 and make no claim about all students.

`responses.csv` holds the anonymous answers (timestamps removed). `python3 docs/survey/summarise.py` reproduces every count below.

## Results (n = 21)

| Question | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Most deadlines and tests in a single week | 3 deadlines: 11. 4: 4. 2: 3. **5 or more: 3.** So **18 of 21 had three or more**, and 7 had four or more |
| Hours spent in that busiest week, outside classes | Under 5: 2. 5 to 10: 6. 10 to 15: 3. 15 to 20: 6. 20 to 30: 1. **More than 30: 3.** Ten of 21 said 15 hours or more |
| Deadline clashes made you submit work you knew wasn't your best | **20 of 21 yes** (once or twice: 13, often: 7). No: 1 |
| Found out late about something not shown on Moodle | **16 of 21 yes** (more than once: 10, once: 6). No: 5 |
| An assignment due before feedback on the previous one in the same module | **17 of 21 yes** (sometimes: 13, often: 4). No: 4 |
| How useful would CrunchRadar be (described in the question) | **20 of 21** said useful: very useful 11, somewhat useful 9. Not useful: 1 |
| Would you read a Monday email with your week's workload | **14 yes**, 7 maybe, 0 no |

## Limits we want to be upfront about
- 21 people is a small, informal sample. Each person is about 5 percentage points.
- The two usefulness questions describe CrunchRadar first, so they show interest, not proof that people would use it.
- Self-reported hours are rough: the answers are ranges.
